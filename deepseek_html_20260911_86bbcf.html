Academic Profile

Nonlinear PDEs & spatial epidemic modeling

Professor specializing in nonlinear partial differential equations and spatial epidemic modeling. My research focuses on reaction–diffusion systems with nonlocal dispersal, age-structured models, and delay-differential equations, with applications in epidemiology and ecological dynamics. Alongside research, I teach numerical analysis, differential equations, and mathematical modeling, and have supervised graduate students working on epidemic and population-dynamics models.

Figures in mathematics

Mathematicians Who Shaped the Field

A few thinkers whose ideas continue to run through modern mathematics and mathematical modeling.

Évariste Galois 1811 – 1832 · French

Founder of what became known as Galois theory, linking group theory to the solvability of polynomial equations by radicals. He wrote most of his foundational work as a teenager and died at twenty, the night before a duel — leaving behind ideas that reshaped abstract algebra.

"Do not weep, Alfred! I need all my courage to die at the age of twenty."From his last letter to Auguste Chevalier, 1832

Henri Poincaré 1854 – 1912 · French

Often called "The Last Universalist" for his reach across mathematics, physics, and philosophy of science. A pioneer of topology and dynamical systems, his work on the three-body problem anticipated modern chaos theory.

"Mathematics is the art of giving the same name to different things."Henri Poincaré

Carl Friedrich Gauss 1777 – 1855 · German

One of history's most influential mathematicians, with foundational contributions to number theory, statistics, differential geometry, and astronomy. Nicknamed "Princeps mathematicorum" — the Prince of Mathematicians.

"Mathematics is the queen of the sciences."Carl Friedrich Gauss

Emmy Noether 1882 – 1935 · German

Developed foundational results in abstract algebra and Noether's theorem, linking symmetries in physics to conservation laws. Regarded as one of the most important mathematicians of the 20th century, despite facing significant institutional barriers as a woman in academia.

"The most significant creative mathematical genius thus far produced since the higher education of women began."Albert Einstein, on Emmy Noether, 1935

Leonhard Euler 1707 – 1783 · Swiss

One of the most prolific mathematicians in history, contributing to analysis, graph theory, mechanics, and optics. He introduced much of modern mathematical notation, including the symbols e, i, and f(x).

"Mathematicians have tried in vain to this day to discover some order in the sequence of prime numbers, and we have reason to believe that it is a mystery into which the human mind will never penetrate."Leonhard Euler

Isaac Newton 1643 – 1727 · English

Laid the foundations of classical mechanics and calculus. His work Philosophiæ Naturalis Principia Mathematica revolutionized physics and mathematics, and his laws of motion and universal gravitation dominated scientific thought for centuries.

"If I have seen further it is by standing on the shoulders of Giants."Isaac Newton, letter to Robert Hooke, 1675

Srinivasa Ramanujan 1887 – 1920 · Indian

A self-taught mathematical genius who made extraordinary contributions to number theory, infinite series, and continued fractions. His collaboration with G. H. Hardy produced some of the most remarkable results in mathematics.

"An equation means nothing to me unless it expresses a thought of God."Srinivasa Ramanujan

Hypatia of Alexandria c. 350 – 415 · Greek

A mathematician, astronomer, and philosopher in Roman Egypt. She was the first known woman mathematician and made significant contributions to geometry and astronomy. Her tragic death marked the end of an era of classical learning.

"Reserve your right to think, for even to think wrongly is better than not to think at all."Hypatia of Alexandria
